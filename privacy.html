01 — Scope

This page is about Cura.

This policy applies specifically to Cura, the Android phone cleaner built by Celtaury, an independent software company based in Indonesia. It covers every place Cura is distributed: Google Play, GitHub Releases, and any direct APK we publish.

It explains what Cura does on your device, what never leaves it on its own, and who we share anything with (nobody). Future Celtaury products will publish their own policies, linked from this site — this page deliberately does not speak for them.

02 — The short version

Cura can’t phone home.

The strongest guarantee on this page isn’t a promise — it’s architecture. Cura does not request the INTERNET permission, and Android enforces that at the operating-system level: the app cannot connect to the internet on its own. No photo, no file, no statistic, no identifier can be sent from Cura to us or to anyone else.

Two things sit outside that guarantee, both spelled out below: Android’s automatic backup (section 04) and links you choose to open yourself (section 05).

There are no accounts and no sign-ups. There is no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no affiliate links inside the app. Every claim here is verifiable, because Cura is open source under the Apache-2.0 license: the complete code lives at github.com/celtaury/cura.

03 — Permissions

What Cura asks for, and why.

Cura reads files only to show you what might be worth cleaning, and deletes only what you approve. It requests storage-related permissions — and nothing else.

  • READ_MEDIA_IMAGES
    READ_MEDIA_VIDEO
    android 13+

    Scans your gallery for old screenshots, duplicates, WhatsApp leftovers and large files, so it can suggest what to clean.

  • READ_MEDIA_AUDIO
    android 13+

    The same scan, for audio files.

  • READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED
    android 14+

    Lets you grant Cura access to only the photos you choose, instead of the whole library.

  • READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE
    android 12 and below

    The older storage permission, requested only on older Android versions.

  • INTERNET — not requested

    The absence of this permission is the guarantee in section 02. You can confirm it yourself: Android Settings → Apps → Cura → Permissions, or check the manifest on GitHub.

All permissions are granted by you in Android and can be revoked at any time from Android Settings → Apps → Cura → Permissions. Without them Cura simply can’t scan — it will never try to compensate by other means.

04 — Storage & deletion

Everything stays on your side.

Cura keeps scan results and your saved stats in the app’s private storage, on your device, inside Android’s sandbox. We never receive a copy of any of it — there is no server on our side to receive one.

One exception, spelled out honestly: automatic backup is switched on for Cura. Your device may include Cura’s local data — its small database of scan results and settings — in Android backups made to your own Google account, or in a direct device-to-device transfer when you move to a new phone. Those backups are managed by Google and Android under their own policies; Celtaury never receives a copy, and a backup never contains your photos or files — only Cura’s own stats and settings. You can turn backup off in your device settings at any time.

Deleting Cura removes all of it on your device permanently. Nothing survives on our servers — because there are none. Want a deletion request on record anyway? Email us or open a GitHub issue, and we’ll confirm in writing that we hold nothing about you.

05 — Sharing

We share your data with no one.

No ad networks, no analytics providers, no data brokers, no social platforms. Cura has no way to send data to any of them, so there is no third party to list.

The one situation where data leaves the app is entirely yours: if you tap a link to an external website — for example our donation page or the GitHub repository — your browser opens that site directly. Cura sends nothing beyond the visit you chose to make, and those websites are operated by third parties under their own privacy policies, not this one.

One boundary, so nothing here contradicts itself: this policy covers Cura, not the stores it lives on. Google Play and GitHub collect and process their own data under their own policies — for example, Google Play shows us aggregate crash statistics and install counts for people who opted in to sharing usage & diagnostics with Google. Those platforms never hand your personal data to us.

06 — Your rights

Your rights, stated plainly.

Laws like the GDPR and Indonesia’s Personal Data Protection Law give you rights to access, correct, export and delete your personal data. We satisfy all of them the short way: Celtaury holds no personal data about anyone, so there is nothing for us to hand over, correct, or delete. Deleting Cura erases everything instantly and permanently.

If you contact us anyway, we will confirm in writing that we hold nothing about you — and help however we can.

07 — Children

Nothing to collect, from anyone.

Cura is not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your jurisdiction), and it does not ask for an age, a name, contact details, or any other personal information — from children or from anyone else. Since the app collects no data at all, it cannot collect children’s data.

08 — Changes

If this ever changes, you’ll know first.

If a future version of Cura ever requires a new permission or a different approach to data, we will update this page and the date above before that version is released, and explain the change in the release notes. Between updates, the open-source repository remains the source of truth.
